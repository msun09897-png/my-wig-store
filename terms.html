— Wig Buying Guide —

How to choose a human hair wig

A practical guide to lace, density, length, texture, color, and fit.

The right wig is the one that fits your routine as well as your look. Use the six decisions below to compare styles before checkout, especially if this is your first human-hair wig.

1. Start with the lace construction

13x6 lace fronts offer the widest front-to-back parting area in our collection and suit shoppers who like to change their part. 13x4 lace fronts still create a natural-looking front hairline with a slightly smaller parting area. 5x5 closures focus the lace around the top-center section and can be a practical choice for easy, glueless wear.

For flexible parting, compare The Signature Straight and The Cascade Wave. For a closure construction, see The Bouncy Curl Closure.

2. Choose a density for your preferred fullness

Density describes how full the wig looks. Around 150% density generally gives a lighter, everyday finish. 180% density looks fuller through the lengths, while 200% density creates a more voluminous result. Curl pattern and length also affect how full a wig appears, so compare density together with texture rather than on its own.

3. Pick length with texture in mind

Straight hair shows its full measured length. Waves and curls look shorter when worn because the pattern draws the hair upward. If you want a curly style to finish at the same visual point as a straight style, you may prefer a longer measurement. Short bobs are lighter and easy to manage; longer styles offer more styling options but usually need more detangling and storage care.

4. Match texture to your routine

  • Straight: polished, easy to brush, and versatile for sleek looks.
  • Body wave: soft movement with a looser, styled finish.
  • Deep or water wave: more visible texture that benefits from gentle detangling and moisture.
  • Curly: high volume and definition; finger-detangle to help preserve the pattern.

5. Consider color and maintenance

Natural black is an easy everyday option. Blonde, highlighted, and burgundy shades make a stronger statement and may require extra care to maintain tone and shine. Human hair can respond differently to heat and color processing, so always perform a strand test first, use a heat protectant, and begin with a low temperature.

6. Check cap details before buying

Review the product page for adjustable straps, combs, elastic bands, and the listed construction. A secure fit should feel stable without painful pressure. If you are unsure about lace type, color, or the best length for your goal, email customer care before checkout.
