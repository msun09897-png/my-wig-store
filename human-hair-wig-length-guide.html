Human Hair Wig Length Guide

How human hair wig length is measured

Length is generally measured from the crown toward the ends with the hair stretched straight. Straight hair therefore shows more of its stated length when worn. Waves and curls draw upward, so they usually appear shorter even when their measured length is the same. Your height, neck length and torso proportions also change where the ends fall.

Short bob and 10–12 inch wigs

Short styles emphasize shape and keep the ends away from much of your clothing. The exact landing point varies by cut and wearer. Compare the fixed short Water Wave Bob, the 10- and 12-inch Platinum Bob, and the fixed 12-inch Burgundy Bob Closure.

14–16 inch wigs

This range can sit around the shoulders or extend below them depending on the wearer and texture. A 16-inch curly wig will look shorter than a 16-inch straight wig because of curl formation. Our fixed 16-inch Bouncy Curl Closure is a useful example, while the Cascade Wave begins at 16 inches.

18–20 inch wigs

These lengths provide more visible movement and styling room without reaching the longest end of the collection. The 1B/27 Honey Noir Straight is available in 18 and 20 inches. Straight texture displays its length more directly than a wave at the same measurement.

24–28 inch wigs

Longer styles create a stronger visual statement and require additional detangling and drying time. Ends also rub against clothing more often. The Signature Straight reaches 24 inches, while selected Cascade Wave options reach 28 inches.

Choose length for your routine

  • Start with the texture shown on the product page, not the number alone.
  • Use a soft measuring tape from the crown to estimate where straightened hair may fall on you.
  • Allow for waves and curls to appear shorter in their natural pattern.
  • Choose a shorter style if you want less contact with collars and outerwear.
  • Choose a longer style only if you are comfortable with the extra care it needs.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a curly 16-inch wig look shorter than a straight one?

The curl pattern draws the hair upward. The stated measurement reflects the hair extended rather than the resting curl.

Does the same length fall in the same place on everyone?

No. Height, torso length, neck length, cap placement and texture all affect the worn result.
